# Video script (5 minutes)

Narration for the 10-slide deck "Pricing across two markets (team CST)"; the same text is in each slide's speaker
notes. About 670 words: roughly 4 min 45 s at a steady pace. If you run long, shorten slide 6.

| Slide | Time | Narration |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 Pricing across two markets | 0:00 | We're team CST. Our task was to price motor, home buildings and home contents in a developed market, then carry that pricing into a new, emerging market with far less data. In the next five minutes: the approach, the results, how we adapted to the new market, and how AI was used and checked. |
| 2 The task | 0:20 | Both markets share one schema. The developed market gives four years and 163,000 claims; the emerging market gives two years and about 9,000. Each test row needs its expected 2025 ultimate claim cost, scored by Tweedie deviance. The twist in 2025: the insurer takes on a broker portfolio, so three quarters of the emerging book is new business, against 13% in training. |
| 3 Data quality | 0:50 | We cleaned both markets with one rule set and counted every fix in a QA log: exposure recorded in days, sums insured a thousand times too large, coded construction types, free-text occupations, and claims outside their policy period. One field leaked the answer: customer service contacts average 3.4 on rows with a claim and 0.4 elsewhere, while test rows look claim-free, so we excluded it. |
| 4 Part 1 | 1:20 | In Part 1 we first developed claims to ultimate: late reporting by line, peril and year, and a structural break in bodily-injury severity in 2023. Severity is trended per peril and large claims are capped, with the excess returned as a loading. Then two Tweedie gradient-boosting models and a frequency–severity pair per line, blended on held-out years. That placed us seventh of fifty-one on the private leaderboard, up from twentieth on the public one. |
| 5 Part 2 pipeline | 1:50 | For the emerging market, two years of data cannot support a full rating model. So the developed market supplies the shape of risk: boosted models per line and per peril, trained only on fields both markets share. The emerging rows then re-fit what they can bear, with Tweedie GLMs that take those priors as offsets. A final calibration sets the new-business loading, the large-loss loading and the line levels for 2025. |
| 6 What the emerging data re-estimates | 2:20 | Here is what the emerging data changed. Open bodily-injury claims are developed by 1.35 and 1.40, borrowed from the developed market. Theft and glass relativities are steeper here. Gender, recorded only in this market, lowers motor cost for women. And because there is no credit band, we use affluence from the marketing segment: budget customers cost 39% more on contents. New business and large losses are calibrated by credibility and by peril mix. |
| 7 Every change | 2:55 | Every change had to improve local validation on three schemes before it reached the leaderboard. Reserve development and the peril-weighted loading both carried over. The pooled model looked better locally but lost on the leaderboard, and its gain came from one heavy year, so we reverted it. Our final model is v7, with the affluence term: its public score is within the noise of the public sample, so local validation decided. |
| 8 Guardrails | 3:25 | The guardrails live in the code. A run stops on any change in row counts or ids, or on a shift in policy timing, and levels outside a band are flagged. Every submission goes through a checker and a ledger that ties its public score to the local validation and the data hashes. Thirty-three tests run on each build, and the final file reproduces byte for byte. |
| 9 AI workflow | 3:50 | Claude Code did the exploration, wrote the pipeline and the tests, and ran every experiment. But a proposal counted for nothing until held-out data agreed. The evidence rejected three AI ideas: a discount term whose gain came from 187 rows with no claims, the pooled model, and reweighting to the 2025 mix. The same checks caught two AI bugs. The actuary approved every submission and made every final call. |
| 10 Uncertainty and price | 4:20 | Some uncertainty remains. Buildings swings by about a quarter between years on a handful of fire claims, the 2025 book is mostly a bulk transfer, and reserve development is borrowed from the developed market. On the emerging expense basis, technical premiums for our reference policies run two to four times the written premiums, so current prices sit well below cost. Thank you. |
